Study guide

The course starts with predictions for individual states and ends with predictions for distributions. Follow the chapter order on a first reading. Each chapter links its recordings, develops the mathematical details, and ends with worked exercises and a computational check.

Continuous dynamics

Start with the bead in Chapter 1. Predict its motion from the vector field before solving for time. In Chapter 2, sketch how the local stable and unstable directions continue into the nonlinear flow. Then study the Hamiltonian chapters with two questions in mind. Which functions stay constant along an orbit? Which geometric structure survives the evolution of a whole ensemble?

Changes in qualitative behavior

For every bifurcation, inventory the invariant states and their stability on each side of the parameter value. For an orbit, keep the return map distinct from the continuous trajectory used to define it. Revisit the two quiz recordings after trying the official problems and the independent exercises.

Discrete and statistical dynamics

Track one orbit under an iterated map, then track a distribution of initial states under the same rule. Compare what an invariant density says with what a single orbit can establish. In the stochastic chapters, write down which variables are random, what is conditioned on, and what physical limit produces the transition or transport model.

Prerequisites

Multivariable calculus, eigenvalues and eigenvectors, introductory mechanics, and basic probability suffice to start. The early chapters define the dynamical vocabulary. Working the labs requires Python and the dependencies in requirements.txt.
