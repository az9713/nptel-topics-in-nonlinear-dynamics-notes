Hamiltonian flows and phase-space volume

L5 L6

An oscillator simulation predicts that the spread of possible initial conditions grows at every step, even though the oscillator is isolated. The same calculation shows a steady rise in energy. Before shortening the time step, determine what the continuous model preserves and whether the numerical update respects any of those structures. An oscillator is a small enough system to answer both questions exactly.

The Hamiltonian couples a coordinate to its conjugate momentum (L5 05:26)

In local canonical coordinates, the state is \(z=(q,p)\in\mathbb R^{2n}\). Here \(q=(q_1,\ldots,q_n)\) denotes the generalized coordinates, \(p=(p_1,\ldots,p_n)\) their conjugate momenta, and \(n\) is the number of degrees of freedom. The scalar function \(H(q,p,t)\) defines Hamilton's equations

\[\dot q_i=\partial_{p_i} H,\qquad \dot p_i=-\partial_{q_i} H .\]

For a particle of mass \(m>0\) under a position-dependent force \(-\nabla V(q)\), take \(H=\|p\|^2/(2m)+V(q)\), with \(V\) its potential energy and \(\|\cdot\|\) the Euclidean norm. The first equation gives \(p=m\dot q\) and the second gives \(\dot p=-\nabla V(q)\). A more general momentum definition comes from a Lagrangian \(L(q,\dot q,t)\), a scalar function of coordinates and velocities that generates the Euler–Lagrange equation \(d(\partial_{\dot q}L)/dt=\partial_q L\). Define \(p=\partial_{\dot q}L\). Where the velocity-to-momentum map is locally invertible, put \(H(q,p,t)=p\cdot \dot q-L(q,\dot q,t)\) using the inverse velocity. In its differential, the \(p\cdot d\dot q\) term from the product cancels \(\partial_{\dot q}L\cdot d\dot q\). This leaves

\[dH=\dot q\cdot dp-\partial_q L\cdot dq-\partial_t L\,dt,\]

which gives the two Hamilton equations along the Euler–Lagrange trajectory. The inverse is a hypothesis. Some Lagrangians have constrained momenta and require a separate treatment. Lecture 5, particle energy. Tong, Hamiltonian formalism

The oscillator used throughout is a dimensionless unit-mass system with unit angular frequency, \(H=(p^2+q^2)/2\). A state \((q,p)\) has velocity vector \((p,-q)\) perpendicular to the gradient \((q,p)\). A gradient flow generated by \(H\) would run directly across its levels. This difference generalizes. Write \(I_n\) for the \(n\)-dimensional identity, \(0\) for the zero block, and define

\[J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_n\\-I_n&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \dot z=J\nabla H.\]

Since \(J^T=-J\), any real vector \(a\) satisfies \(a^TJa=0\). This is why \(H\)'s gradient contributes no directional derivative along its own Hamiltonian field. The oscillator moves on circles with time period \(2\pi\). In physical coordinates with \(H=p^2/(2m)+m\omega^2q^2/2\), the circles become ellipses and the angular frequency is \(\omega\).

The Poisson bracket generates observable dynamics (L5 23:45)

For differentiable observables \(A(q,p,t)\) and \(B(q,p,t)\), their Poisson bracket differentiates only in the state variables:

\[\{A,B\}=(\nabla A)^T J\nabla B =\sum_{i=1}^n(\partial_{q_i}A\,\partial_{p_i}B -\partial_{p_i}A\,\partial_{q_i}B).\]

It satisfies \(\{q_i,p_j\}=\delta_{ij}\), equal to \(1\) for \(i=j\) and \(0\) otherwise, and vanishes between two coordinates or two momenta. Fixing \(B\) gives a directional derivative along the field \(J\nabla B\). Linearity follows from the gradient's linearity. The skew matrix gives antisymmetry. The product rule gives \(\{AB,C\}=A\{B,C\}+B\{A,C\}\). The Jacobi identity for smooth functions follows by expanding the cyclic sum \(\{A,\{B,C\}\}+\{B,\{C,A\}\}+\{C,\{A,B\}\}\). Terms containing the second derivatives of each of \(A,B,C\) cancel in pairs because mixed partials commute and the two instances have opposite signs from swapping a pair in the outer bracket. This identity lets the Poisson bracket serve as a Lie bracket. Lecture 5, bracket properties

Along a trajectory the chain rule yields

\[\frac{dA}{dt}=\partial_t A+\{A,H\}.\]

An observable with this sum identically zero is a first integral. In particular \(dH/dt=\partial_t H\), so an autonomous Hamiltonian (one with no explicit time dependence) is a first integral. An explicitly time-dependent observable can still be conserved. For the dimensionless free particle \(H=p^2/2\), set \(A=q-pt\). Its bracket with \(H\) equals \(p\), cancelling \(\partial_t A=-p\). It records the intercept of the straight trajectory at the chosen time origin. For the oscillator \(\{q,H\}=p\) and \(\{p,H\}=-q\). Individually neither observable is constant. Their contributions cancel for \(A=H\). Lecture 5, observable derivative and constants

Volume preservation is a constraint on an entire flow map (L5 43:37)

For a twice continuously differentiable Hamiltonian, the divergence of its canonical field is

\[\nabla\cdot (J\nabla H) =\sum_{i=1}^n\left(\partial_{q_i}\partial_{p_i}H -\partial_{p_i}\partial_{q_i}H\right)=0.\]

The mixed partials cancel at every state. The determinant identity of Chapter 2 extends without change to \(2n\) dimensions, since the flow Jacobian satisfies the same matrix differential equation. Hence the canonical phase volume \(dq_1\cdots dq_n\,dp_1\cdots dp_n\) is preserved under the flow wherever defined. This phase-volume statement, commonly called Liouville's theorem in Hamiltonian mechanics, also holds for smooth time-dependent \(H\). Its argument uses state derivatives at each fixed time. Conservation of the value of \(H\) itself still requires \(\partial_t H=0\). Lecture 6, phase-volume recap

In the isolated oscillator every initial ellipse rotates forever and all initial areas are preserved. A saddle shows what the determinant can conceal. Take \(H=qp\) for one canonical pair. Hamilton's equations give \((q(t),p(t))=(e^t q_0,e^{-t}p_0)\). A small axis-aligned rectangle stretches in \(q\) and contracts in \(p\) at reciprocal rates. An attracting equilibrium with an open basin would violate preservation of area or volume. To see this, take a finite-positive-volume subset of that basin and a ball around the equilibrium of smaller volume. As time increases the fraction of the subset whose image lies in the ball tends to \(1\) by pointwise convergence and bounded convergence of indicator functions. A volume-preserving injective flow cannot fit that much volume inside the ball.

Conversely, a volume-preserving field in dimension four can fail to be Hamiltonian for a specified \(J\). In coordinates \((q_1,q_2,p_1,p_2)\), consider \(\dot q_1=q_1,\ \dot q_2=-q_2,\ \dot p_1=\dot p_2=0\). Its divergence vanishes. Hamilton's equations would require \(\partial_{q_1}H=\partial_{q_2}H=0\) and \(\partial_{p_1}H=q_1\) on an open set. These requirements have incompatible mixed derivatives. Volume preservation gives one scalar restriction and Hamiltonian form also constrains the full pairing of coordinates with momenta.

Canonical transformations preserve brackets and Hamilton's equations (L6 19:06)

For a time-independent smooth invertible coordinate change \(Z=\Phi(z)=(Q,P)\), write \(M=D\Phi(z)\). A change is canonical when its new coordinate brackets equal the old ones, or equivalently \(MJM^T=J\). For instance \((Q,P)=(a q,p/a)\) for a nonzero scalar \(a\) is canonical for one pair: \(\{a q,p/a\}=1\). A uniform scale on both variables by \(a\) multiplies their bracket by \(a^2\). Choosing which variable is conjugate to which therefore matters when rescaling a model.

Write \(K(Z)=H(\Phi^{-1}(Z))\). The gradient relation is \(\nabla_z H=M^T\nabla_Z K\). The transformed dynamics are then \(\dot Z=M J M^T\nabla_Z K=J\nabla_Z K\). The oscillator in the rescaled variables has \(K(Q,P)=(Q^2/a^2+a^2P^2)/2\). Its level shapes change, and its period stays \(2\pi\). Canonical form is preserved with the transformed function.

Commuting first integrals restrict where the flow can move (L6 39:22)

Suppose the autonomous \(H\) is one of \(n\) smooth first integrals \(F_1,\ldots,F_n\), defined on an open neighborhood of a compact connected component of their joint level. Assume their gradients are independent at every point of the component and the functions are in involution on the neighborhood, meaning \(\{F_i,F_j\}=0\) there for every pair. Independence is a rank condition. Involution is a separate differential identity, satisfied even by some pairs that are functionally dependent. The implicit function theorem gives dimension \(2n-n=n\) for this regular component. Each vector \(J\nabla F_i\) is tangent to it because \(dF_j(J\nabla F_i)=\{F_j,F_i\}=0\). Their independence follows from invertibility of \(J\), so they span its tangent space. The Liouville–Arnold theorem adds that the component is a torus, and that nearby canonical coordinates can be chosen so only \(n\) action variables appear in \(H\). Dunajski, integrable systems, Theorem 1.2.2

For two uncoupled copies of the oscillator, \(F_i=(p_i^2+q_i^2)/2\) have disjoint coordinate pairs, so their bracket is zero. They are independent on the joint level \(F_1=c_1>0\), \(F_2=c_2>0\), where neither pair's gradient vanishes. The product of two circles is a torus, and the total Hamiltonian \(H=F_1+F_2\) generates motion on it. Either \((F_1,F_2)\) or \((H,F_2)\) provides the required integrals. At \(c_1=0\) the first circle collapses and rank is lost. A regular compact component gives a local theorem around that torus. It supplies neither a proof of global coordinates across singular levels nor a recipe guaranteed to locate the integrals. Chapter 4 develops action–angle variables and nontrivial examples.

Laboratory

The oscillator experiment checks two fixed update maps on the unit oscillator. With dimensionless time step \(h\), forward Euler gives \((q+h p,p-h q)\). Squaring and adding shows it multiplies energy by \(1+h^2\) at each step. A kick followed by a drift gives \(p'=p-hq\) and \(q'=q+hp'\). Both operations are shears of determinant \(1\), and direct substitution shows they preserve the modified energy \((q^2+p^2-hqp)/2\). For \(0<h<2\) it is positive definite. Thus the kick–drift trajectory stays bounded in exact arithmetic, although its physical oscillator energy can oscillate with the phase. The code checks both exact update laws over 200 steps from the fixed state \((1,0)\). It also verifies preservation of canonical brackets at the matrix level.

Exercises

  1. For \(L(q,\dot q)=m\dot q^2/2-kq^2/2\) with \(m,k>0\), perform the Legendre transformation and recover the second-order equation.
  2. Take \(H(q,p)=p^2/2+\alpha q^4/4\) with a constant \(\alpha>0\). Compute \(\{q p,H\}\) and explain why \(qp\) need not be conserved on a periodic orbit.
  3. For \(H(q,p,t)=p^2/2+k(t)q^2/2\), derive the instantaneous rates of change of both \(H\) and the determinant of the flow map, assuming \(k\) is smooth.
  4. Check whether \(Q=q,\ P=p+\beta q^2\) with constant \(\beta\) is canonical. Express the unit oscillator Hamiltonian in \(Q,P\).
  5. In a two-oscillator system let \(F\) be the first oscillator's energy. Show that \(F\) and \(F^2\) commute and fail to give independent constraints. Give a pair that passes both conditions on a product of two positive-energy circles.
  6. At \(h=1/2\), compute the first forward-Euler and kick–drift steps for \((q,p)=(1,0)\). Check the physical energy and the modified energy for the kick–drift step.
Worked solutions
  1. The momentum is \(p=m\dot q\), so \(\dot q=p/m\). Substitution gives \(H=p^2/m-(p^2/(2m)-kq^2/2)=p^2/(2m)+kq^2/2\). Hamilton's equations are \(\dot q=p/m\) and \(\dot p=-kq\), giving \(m\ddot q=-kq\) after differentiation.
  2. The gradients are \((p,q)\) and \((\alpha q^3,p)\). Therefore \(\{qp,H\}=p^2-\alpha q^4\), which at a nonzero turning point \((q,0)\) is negative. Periodicity ensures that the net change after a full period vanishes. The observable can vary over the period.
  3. The antisymmetric bracket with itself vanishes, so \(dH/dt=k'(t)q^2/2\). The state field is \((p,-k(t)q)\), whose state divergence is zero for each \(t\). Consequently the determinant stays \(1\) and its time derivative is zero.
  4. The bracket is \(\partial_q Q\,\partial_p P-\partial_p Q\,\partial_q P=1\). The map has the inverse \(q=Q,\ p=P-\beta Q^2\), giving \(K=[Q^2+(P-\beta Q^2)^2]/2\). Keeping \(\beta\) fixed makes the transformation time independent.
  5. Bilinearity and the product rule give \(\{F,F^2\}=2F\{F,F\}=0\). However \(\nabla F^2=2F\nabla F\), so the common levels reduce to one independent condition. The individual oscillator energies commute because they involve disjoint coordinate pairs, and each gradient is nonzero on its positive-energy circle.
  6. Forward Euler yields \((1,-1/2)\) and energy \(5/8\). Kick–drift yields \((3/4,-1/2)\) and energy \((9/16+1/4)/2=13/32\). The modified energy adds \(-(1/2)(3/4)(-1/2)/2=3/32\) to give \(1/2\), its initial value.
