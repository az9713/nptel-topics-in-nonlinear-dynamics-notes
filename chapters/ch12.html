Chapter 12 · Lectures 26–27

Fractal sets, torus maps, and dynamical diagnostics

L26 L27

Suppose a simulator produces a tangled image after a few iterations and also repeats exactly after a long run. Which observation describes the model, and which describes the arithmetic? The Arnold cat map makes this question tractable because the map has a closed-form derivative and any rational grid can be enumerated exactly. The continued-fraction map then gives a complementary example in which an expanding map distorts the stationary density. The quiz tests which diagnostics survive changes of model.

Counting copies before counting samples (L26 00:14)

An allowed sequence of ternary digits using only \(0\) and \(2\) specifies a point of the middle-third Cantor set. At scale \(3^{-k}\) the first \(k\) digits leave \(2^k\) intervals, with endpoint conventions having no effect on the exponent. Thus the box dimension is \(\log2/\log3\). For the Sierpiński gasket the construction keeps three corner triangles at scale \(1/2\) and repeats inside each. These triangles intersect at vertices only, and a square of side comparable to \(2^{-k}\) can meet at most a bounded number of their interiors or neighboring vertices. Counting the pieces gives \(\log3/\log2\). Keeping the four half-size corners of a tetrahedron gives exponent \(\log4/\log2=2\) by the same argument. The last set sits in three dimensions, with vanishing limiting volume since the total volume fraction after \(k\) steps is \((4/8)^k\). Box dimension can be an integer even when the limiting set differs sharply from a smooth surface.

The cat map stretches without changing area (L26 13:53)

The two-dimensional torus \(\mathbb T^2=\mathbb R^2/\mathbb Z^2\) identifies points differing by an integer vector. Represent one equivalence class by coordinates in \([0,1)^2\). The cat map is the transformation \(\mathbf x\mapsto A\mathbf x\bmod\mathbb Z^2\) with

\[A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}.\]

Because \(A\) has integer entries, changing the representative by an integer vector yields the same output class. The determinant is one, the inverse has integer entries, and the Jacobian scales area by one. Thus wrapping the parallelogram back into the square preserves the uniform probability measure. The map is invertible even though plotting in one square splits wrapped shapes at the edges.

Its trace and determinant give the characteristic polynomial \(\lambda^2-3\lambda+1\). The eigenvalues \(\lambda_\pm=(3\pm\sqrt5)/2\) obey \(\lambda_-=\lambda_+^{-1}\), so the factors in the two eigenvector directions after \(n\) steps are respectively \(\lambda_\pm^n\). The Lyapunov exponents, defined as asymptotic logarithmic stretching per time step for tangent perturbations in these directions, are \(\log\lambda_\pm\). Taking the separation of two wrapped positions in a fundamental square would keep it bounded and conceal this derivative-based stretching. The product of stretching factors equals one. The curve tangent to the expanding direction through a point wraps repeatedly around the torus, while contraction along the other eigendirection retains the same total area.

Each rational point lies in a finite grid with some common denominator \(m\), and the integer matrix acts bijectively on that grid, so every rational point is periodic. If a point has period dividing \(n\), then \((A^n-I)\mathbf x\) is an integer vector, where \(I\) is the \(2\times2\) identity. Since \(A^n-I\) is an invertible integer matrix over the reals, its inverse has rational entries. Thus every periodic point of the torus map is rational. There are exactly \(|\det(A^n-I)|\) solutions modulo \(\mathbb Z^2\): integer vectors of right-hand sides label solutions with equivalence modulo the sublattice \((A^n-I)\mathbb Z^2\), whose lattice index equals the absolute determinant. That index follows from the area of the fundamental parallelogram. For \(n=1\) there is one solution. For \(n=2\), \(A^2-I\) has rows \((1,3)\) and \((3,4)\) and determinant \(-5\). Four more points give two exact period-two cycles. Finite grid repetition is built into the arithmetic even when the underlying map has positive tangent expansion.

Continued fractions and the invariant Gauss density (L26 38:55)

For irrational \(x\in(0,1)\) define \(G(x)=1/x-\lfloor1/x\rfloor\) and digit \(a_1=\lfloor1/x\rfloor\). The identity \(x=1/(a_1+G(x))\) starts the continued-fraction algorithm. Iteration gives the sequence of positive integer digits \(\{a_j\}_{j\ge1}\), with \(a_j=\lfloor1/G^{j-1}(x)\rfloor\). Rational inputs terminate when the remainder reaches zero. A fixed point with repeated digit \(a\) solves \(x=1/(a+x)\), or \(x=(\sqrt{a^2+4}-a)/2\). Thus the inverse golden ratio has digit one forever. The derivative on a branch is \(-1/x^2\), so fixed points in \((0,1)\) are repelling.

On the branch with digit \(k\) the inverse of \(G\) is \(y_k(x)=1/(k+x)\), with absolute derivative \((k+x)^{-2}\). Conservation of mass under change of variables gives the image density of any initial density \(\rho\):

\[(\mathcal P\rho)(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho(1/(k+x))}{(k+x)^2}.\]

To check a candidate stationary density \(\rho_G(x)=1/[(1+x)\log2]\), first integrate over \((0,1)\) to get one. Substitution into the transfer operator reduces each term to \(\big[(k+x)^{-1}-(k+x+1)^{-1}\big]/\log2\). All terms telescope to \(\rho_G(x)\). The digit probability equals the invariant mass of the digit interval:

\[\Pr(a_1=k)=\int_{1/(k+1)}^{1/k}\rho_G(x)\,dx =\frac{\log[(k+1)^2/(k(k+2))]}{\log2}.\]

The computation proves invariance and gives a stationary marginal. Typical time frequencies require ergodicity, and convergence from other densities requires more than the fixed-point identity. Doeblin studies further metric properties of the digits. Doeblin (1940)

Mean tangent expansion of the Gauss map (L26 60:00)

The invariant average of the logarithmic derivative magnitude is

\[\int_0^1 \rho_G(x)\log|G'(x)|\,dx =\frac{2}{\log2}\int_0^1\frac{-\log x}{1+x}\,dx =\frac{\pi^2}{6\log2}.\]

For the second equality, expand \(1/(1+x)\) as a geometric series and use \(\int_0^1 x^{k-1}(-\log x)\,dx=1/k^2\). Interchange of series and integral follows from absolute summability of these integrated terms. Summing gives \(\sum_{k\ge1}(-1)^{k-1}/k^2=(1-2^{-1})\zeta(2)=\pi^2/12\), using the classical value \(\zeta(2)=\pi^2/6\) of the Riemann zeta function \(\zeta(s)=\sum_{k\ge1}k^{-s}\) for real \(s>1\). If one invokes ergodicity for the Gauss map then the integral is the typical Lyapunov exponent by the ergodic theorem and the derivative chain rule. Special periodic points need not share it. For example the inverse golden ratio has exponent twice the logarithm of the golden ratio, from its own derivative repeated forever.

Quiz 2 puts each diagnostic on trial (L27 00:11)

The official quiz revisits the hypotheses of earlier chapters. The noninvertible constant map collapses all trajectories to one value, so a discarded branch does not by itself give chaos. The typical Lyapunov exponent of the invariant logistic law at \(r=4\) is \(\log2\). At the period-doubling accumulation point the usual exponent for the attractor measure is zero. Special orbits can behave differently. Starting exactly at the critical point gives zero tangent derivative at the first step. Starting at the fixed point \(x=0\) when \(r=4\) gives exponent \(\log4\) along a constant orbit. Baldovin and Robledo, sensitivity at the logistic chaos threshold

A chaotic invariant set can also have smaller box dimension than ambient phase space. As an explicit construction, stretch the horizontal coordinate by two modulo one and update a vertical coordinate by \(y\mapsto(y+2\lfloor2x\rfloor)/3\). After \(k\) steps the vertical coordinate is confined to the \(2^k\) stage-\(k\) Cantor intervals. Its limiting invariant support is a square's horizontal interval times the Cantor set, with box dimension \(1+\log2/\log3<2\) by multiplying the grid counts at side \(3^{-k}\).

When the linear part vanishes, a Lyapunov function can still decide stability. For \(\dot x=-x^3\), \(V=x^2/2\) has derivative \(-x^4\), strictly negative off the origin. At the logistic fixed point \(1-1/r\) the multiplier is \(2-r\). It crosses \(-1\) at \(r=3\), the signature of a flip rather than the crossing of a complex conjugate pair in a Hopf bifurcation. In the autonomous Hamiltonian case the ordinary dissipative Hopf mechanism with an attracting or repelling cycle is excluded by preservation of phase volume. A Hamiltonian-Hopf instability is a distinct phenomenon involving colliding imaginary eigenvalues.

For the damped Duffing case \(\dot x=y\), \(\dot y=x-x^3-y\), the energy \(E=y^2/2-x^2/2+x^4/4\) has \(\dot E=-y^2\). A periodic orbit would integrate a strictly negative contribution unless \(y\) is identically zero, so no nonconstant cycle is possible. The equilibria \((1,0)\) and \((-1,0)\) already disprove global attraction to the origin. For the quiz's singular field, the complex representation of its vector is \(z^{-2}\) for \(z=x+iy\), with \(i^2=-1\). Taking a circle counterclockwise once decreases the vector angle by \(4\pi\), giving winding number \(-2\). For the cubic map \(f(x)=3x-4x^3\), write \(x=\sin\theta\) on \(-\pi/2\le\theta\le\pi/2\). Since \(f(\sin\theta)=\sin3\theta\), reducing \(3\theta\) back to that angle interval gives three affine branches of absolute slope three. Interior periodic orbits away from the folds are repelling. The folds lead to the endpoint cycle, whose original-coordinate derivatives each equal \(-9\). This rules out a stable three-cycle. Finally, the stationary centered telegraph model of Chapter 11 gives the quiz's exponential covariance, with decay rate equal to the sum of the jump rates.

Even-digit decimal sets (L27 20:49)

The even-digit decimal set has five choices per digit, so a scale-\(10^{-k}\) grid sees order \(5^k\) occupied intervals. Its box dimension is \(\log5/\log10\).

An arithmetic averaging protocol hides an oscillatory mode (L27 25:32)

Equalizing two identical glasses replaces their heights by their mean. In one cycle synchronize \(A,B\), then \(B,C\), then \(C,A\). Starting with \((x,y,z)^T\), the first average is \(s=(x+y)/2\), the second is \(t=(s+z)/2\), and the final height of \(A\) and \(C\) is \(u=(s+t)/2\). The cycle matrix is

\[M=\frac18\begin{pmatrix}3&3&2\\2&2&4\\3&3&2\end{pmatrix}.\]

The columns sum to one, so the initial mean \(\bar h=(x_0+y_0+z_0)/3\) is conserved. The vector \(\mathbf 1=(1,1,1)^T\) is fixed. After the first cycle all deviations from the mean have the form \((d,-2d,d)^T\), with \(d=u-\bar h\). Applying \(M\) to this vector multiplies it by \(-1/8\). Hence if \(\mathbf h_1=M\mathbf h_0\),

\[\mathbf h_n=\bar h\mathbf1+(-1/8)^{n-1}(\mathbf h_1-\bar h\mathbf1),\qquad n\ge1.\]

Successive deviations change sign even though each individual operation averages positive heights. The zero mode is the initial difference \((1,-1,0)^T\) erased by the first pair average. A one-step conservation law and a mode calculation answer different parts of the problem: the conserved quantity sets the target and the surviving eigenmode sets the trajectory toward it.

From lag covariance to a spectrum (L27 35:27)

For a real stationary signal \(X_t\) with finite variance and mean \(\bar x\), set \(C(\tau)=\mathbb E[(X_{t+\tau}-\bar x)(X_t-\bar x)]\). Stationarity removes the time origin, and commutativity of the product makes \(C\) even. For integrable covariance, use the two-sided angular-frequency convention \(S(\omega)=\int_{\mathbb R}C(\tau)e^{-i\omega\tau}\,d\tau\). This is the power spectral density. The telegraph covariance \(C(\tau)=\sigma^2e^{-\kappa|\tau|}\), with variance \(\sigma^2\) and decay rate \(\kappa>0\), yields \(S(\omega)=2\sigma^2\kappa/(\kappa^2+\omega^2)\) by integrating the real cosine contribution on the positive half-line. A random-phase sinusoid produces a covariance proportional to a cosine, so its spectrum has point masses at its two angular frequencies under the corresponding spectral-measure convention. A broad empirical spectrum can reflect deterministic dynamics, stochastic forcing, or limited sampling. The spectrum studies second moments and does not identify the full path law. Wiener (1930)

Laboratory

Run the map and quiz laboratory with Python. It counts one fixed point and two exact period-two cycles on the denominator-five grid. It uses rational arithmetic for six cycles of the three-glass protocol and checks the closed form on a case with initial heights \((7,1,8)\) and first result \((5,6,5)\). It also checks the finite Gauss transfer sum plus the exact telescoping tail.

Exercises

  1. Trace a rational point. For the cat map on the denominator-five grid, start at \((1/5,3/5)\) and compute its next two iterates.
  2. Estimate the digit tail exactly. If \(x\) has the Gauss invariant density, find \(\Pr(a_1\ge k)\).
  3. Find a period two continued fraction. Solve for the continued fraction with repeating digits \(1,2\) and show that its orbit really has two distinct points.
  4. Find the finite-time error. For the lab's initial heights, compute the height of glass \(B\) after two cycles from the eigenvalue formula.
  5. Compare spectra. The centered variance of two stationary telegraph models is the same. If one has twice the decay rate, how do their heights at zero frequency and their half-height widths compare?
Worked solutions
  1. In numerator coordinates modulo five, \((1,3)\) maps to \((4,2)\) since \(1+3=4\) and \(1+2\cdot3=7\). The next state is \((1,3)\) since the outputs before reducing are \(6,8\). Dividing by five gives an exact cycle of period two.
  2. The event is \(x\le1/k\) up to endpoints of zero measure. Integration gives \(\log(1+1/k)/\log2\), asymptotic to \(1/(k\log2)\). The large digits are rare but the tail is slow.
  3. Solve \(x=1/(1+1/(2+x))=(2+x)/(3+x)\), giving \(x^2+2x-2=0\) and \(x=\sqrt3-1\). The first digit is one, while the next remainder \(1/(2+x)\) lies between \(1/3\) and \(1/2\) and has next digit two. The second iterate returns to \(x\) by construction and the two points lie in different branches.
  4. The mean is \(16/3\). The first height of \(B\) is \(6\), with deviation \(2/3\). Multiplying by \(-1/8\) gives deviation \(-1/12\) and height \(16/3-1/12=21/4\).
  5. At zero, \(S(0)=2\sigma^2/\kappa\). Increasing \(\kappa\) by two halves this height. Half height occurs at \(|\omega|=\kappa\), so that width doubles. Faster time decay broadens the frequency distribution while preserving the total two-sided spectral mass with this convention.
